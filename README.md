# BUS5001 – NotebookLM Evaluation

## 1. Project Overview

This project evaluates the feasibility of using **Google NotebookLM** as an AI-powered academic research and learning assistant in a university environment.

The evaluation focuses on whether NotebookLM can help students understand complex documents, compare information from multiple sources, and prepare effective study materials.

A realistic academic scenario was developed around **Australia's Sustainability Reporting Framework**, using three official Australian publications.

This project forms part of **Question 4 – Evaluating Cloud-Based Technology** in BUS5001 Assessment 3.

## 2. Academic Scenario

**Scenario:** Preparing an academic briefing and revision materials on Australia's sustainability reporting framework.

A university student needs to understand Australia's climate-related reporting standards, regulatory guidance, and sustainable finance policies.

NotebookLM is evaluated as a tool to support document analysis, source-based research, and assessment preparation.

## 3. Knowledge Base

Three publicly available Australian documents were uploaded into NotebookLM.

| Source | Organisation | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| AASB S2 – Climate-related Disclosures (2024) | Australian Accounting Standards Board | Explains climate-related financial disclosure requirements. |
| Regulatory Guide 280 – Sustainability Reporting (2025) | Australian Securities and Investments Commission | Provides regulatory guidance on sustainability reporting obligations and compliance. |
| Sustainable Finance Roadmap (2024) | Australian Treasury | Outlines Australia's sustainable finance policy priorities and direction. |

## 4. Evaluation Methodology

Three practical tests were conducted using NotebookLM's free access.

Each test evaluated a different academic activity:

| Test | Functionality | Academic Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| T01 | Source-Grounded Question Answering | Understanding reporting requirements through AI-generated explanations and citations. |
| T02 | Multi-Source Comparison | Comparing sustainability reporting standards with regulatory guidance. |
| T03 | Study Guide Generation | Creating revision notes, key concepts, and practice exam questions. |

The outputs were evaluated using five criteria:

1. Accuracy and Relevance
2. Completeness
3. Citation Quality
4. Academic Usefulness
5. Limitations

The evaluation was qualitative and based on the observed outputs, citation checks, and relevance to academic workflows.


## 6. Consolidated Evaluation

| Criterion | Overall Finding |
|---|---|
| Accuracy and Relevance | Outputs were generally aligned with the academic questions and selected sources. |
| Completeness | All three tests produced the main requested content. |
| Citation Quality | Citations were available, but individual supporting claims were not fully verified. |
| Academic Usefulness | NotebookLM supported document understanding, research comparison, and exam preparation. |
| Limitations | Regulatory information may be oversimplified, and users may over-rely on AI-generated explanations. |

## 7. Academic Risks and Limitations

Several limitations were identified during the evaluation:

- **Citation reliability:** Citation markers make sources easier to locate, but do not guarantee that every explanation is fully supported.
- **Oversimplification:** Technical reporting requirements and legal obligations may lose important conditions when summarised.
- **Hallucination risk:** AI-generated outputs may contain unsupported claims, even when based on uploaded documents.
- **Over-reliance:** Students may accept generated summaries without consulting the original readings.
- **Data privacy:** Universities should assess privacy, copyright, document access permissions, and data governance before uploading confidential learning materials.

No confirmed hallucination was established in the three tests. The evaluation identified potential risks and areas requiring further verification.

## 8. Feasibility and Recommendation

The results suggest that NotebookLM is a promising supplementary learning tool for university students, particularly for reading complex documents, comparing sources, and preparing revision materials.

However, this small-scale evaluation does not establish the tool's reliability across different academic subjects or users.

A controlled university pilot is recommended, supported by staff guidance, appropriate document-sharing policies, academic integrity requirements, and human verification of important information.

NotebookLM should support students' learning and critical thinking rather than replace original academic sources.

## 11. Scope of Evaluation

This evaluation was conducted using three public Australian documents and three practical tests.

It did not involve real student records, confidential university data, enterprise integration, or a production deployment.

All conclusions are based on this limited academic experiment.
