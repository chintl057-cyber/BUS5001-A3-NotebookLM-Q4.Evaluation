# Q4 – NotebookLM Evaluation Summary

## 1. Summary of Experimental Results

| Test | Key Findings | Main Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| T01 – Question Answering | Correctly identified the four AASB S2 disclosure areas and provided source citations. | Specific supporting citation passages were not fully verified. |
| T02 – Multi-Source Comparison | Distinguished AASB S2 reporting standards from ASIC RG 280 regulatory guidance. | Some detailed legal interpretations require further verification. |
| T03 – Study Guide Generation | Generated five key concepts, institutional role explanations and three practice exam questions. | Some technical and regulatory details were simplified. |

## 2. Comparative Evaluation

| Criterion | T01 – Q&A | T02 – Comparison | T03 – Study Guide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Accuracy & Relevance | Good | Good | Generally accurate |
| Completeness | Comprehensive | Comprehensive | Comprehensive |
| Citation Quality | Partially verified | Not fully verified | Not fully verified |
| Academic Usefulness | Highly useful | Highly useful | Highly useful |
| Main Limitation | Citation verification | Legal interpretation | Oversimplification |

These qualitative assessments reflect the observed outputs from three tests rather than independently measured accuracy scores.

## 3. Key Strengths

**Source-Based Learning:** NotebookLM generated answers using uploaded academic documents and provided clickable citations, helping users locate relevant source material.

**Research Efficiency:** The tool organised information across multiple documents, making complex sustainability reporting concepts easier to compare.

**Study Support:** NotebookLM transformed lengthy technical materials into structured revision notes and practice exam questions.

## 4. Risks and Limitations

**Citation Reliability:** Citation markers were available, but not all supporting passages were independently verified. Students may incorrectly assume that cited explanations are fully accurate.

**Oversimplification:** Summaries may omit important conditions relating to legal obligations, reporting thresholds and regulatory exemptions.

**Hallucination and Bias:** AI-generated explanations may contain unsupported interpretations. No confirmed hallucination or systematic demographic bias was established during these tests.

**Over-reliance:** Students may rely on generated summaries rather than reading and critically evaluating original materials.

**Privacy and Governance:** University adoption would require appropriate policies for uploading copyrighted, confidential or personal information, including access permissions and data handling controls.

## 5. Feasibility Assessment

| Evaluation Area | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Academic usefulness | Promising for document analysis and revision |
| Technical usability | Successfully demonstrated through three practical tests |
| Output reliability | Encouraging, but not independently established |
| Citation verification | Human checking remains necessary |
| Institutional readiness | Further governance and broader testing required |

## 6. Recommendation

**Google NotebookLM is recommended for a limited university pilot as a supplementary learning tool, rather than immediate institution-wide deployment.**

The pilot should initially use approved academic materials and provide students with guidance on verifying AI-generated information.

Before wider adoption, the university should evaluate the tool across additional academic disciplines, develop privacy and copyright guidance, establish academic integrity expectations, and assess accessibility and student learning outcomes.

NotebookLM should support students' independent research and critical thinking, not replace original academic sources or lecturer guidance.

## 7. Conclusion

Across three tests, NotebookLM demonstrated useful capabilities in question answering, document comparison and study guide generation.

However, the limited test scope and incomplete citation verification mean that these results cannot establish general reliability.

The findings support further controlled evaluation with human oversight and appropriate university governance.
